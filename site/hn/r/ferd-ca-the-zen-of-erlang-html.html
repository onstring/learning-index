The Zen of Erlang

https://ferd.ca/the-zen-of-erlang.html · 中文 (Google 翻译)

Learn Erlang's fault-tolerant design philosophy centered on controlled restarts for system reliability.

Explainer Intermediate Recommended by 3 different commenters across 3 years (2020–2025)

Discussed on Hacker News: The Zen of Erlang (301 points)

What Hacker News says

  • "Let it crash" is largely about restarting systems to known good state in an expanding scope. The Zen of Erlang[1] covers it really well, it's a longer read but well worth it if you want to understand a lot of the design choices of Erlang. Nearly all of the devices you use employ these approaches in one form or another. A watchdog timer[2] is a pretty simple and powerful version of this. Timeouts and retries follow a somewhat similar approach, Erlang just embraces that across the whole language. It really is a fascinating approach to a different design space(latency and reliability over throughput) through the requirements a telecom stack necessitated. [1] https://ferd.ca/the-zen-of-erlang.h

    vvanders, 2021 · comment · in What I learned from Erlang about resiliency in systems design (2019)

  • I recommend https://ferd.ca/the-zen-of-erlang.html starting from "if my configuration file is corrupted, restarting won't fix anything". The tl;dr is it helps with transient bugs.
