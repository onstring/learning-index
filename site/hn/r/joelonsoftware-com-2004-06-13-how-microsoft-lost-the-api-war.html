How Microsoft Lost the API War

Explores Microsoft's strategic missteps in API development and the importance of backward compatibility in software design.

What Hacker News says

  • Joel Spolsky famously praised this policy of backward-compatibility at all costs which he called "The Raymond Chen Camp"[1]. Many agreed with him, but I always thought that Microsoft compatibility ideals were too radical to be real wisdom. At some point the list of features you try to keep compatibility with grows large enough that the Raymond Chen Way becomes unmaintainable. The received wisdom of the 90s is wrong. Most users don't care about compatibility, as Apple's success has clearly shown, and most companies are now out following the Apple road. Large enterprise care about compatibility, and they pay a lot, but this is not a forward-looking market. They'll keep using buying new version
