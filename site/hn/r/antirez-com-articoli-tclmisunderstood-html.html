Tcl the Misunderstood

https://antirez.com/articoli/tclmisunderstood.html · 中文 (Google 翻译)

Learn why Tcl is misunderstood as a toy language and its powerful features as an embedded interpreter.

Explainer Intermediate Recommended by 3 different commenters across 4 years (2013–2022)

Discussed on Hacker News: Tcl the Misunderstood (2006) (182 points)

What Hacker News says

  • Yes, in Tcl everything is basically a string, but has Lisp-like recursive structures: An expression like ` {foo {bar baz}}` evaluates to the string "foo {bar baz}" at first, but then that might be split into two strings "foo" and "bar baz" by the interpreter. I recall Tcl being described as a Lisp without the parenthesis many years ago, which I still think is somewhat apt. Antirez' article "Tcl the Misunderstood" [0] is well worth a read if you want to know more. He also has an interpreter, Picol [1], in 500 lines of C code that is very easy to read and to understand. [0] http://antirez.com/articoli/tclmisunderstood.html [1] http://oldblog.antirez.com/post/picol.html

    wernsey, 2022 · comment · in Tcl Ported to Go

  • Antirez' comments on Tcl are also worth reading: http://antirez.com/articoli/tclmisunderstood.html I think you appreciate Tcl the more you see how it's built under the hood. I'm guessing you're using some crufty EDA stuff though, where you're not seeing the good side of the language, and dealing with a lot of accumulated baggage.
