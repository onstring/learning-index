  • "I'd be more interested on what someone who actually has a lot of experience in using C++ says." I have considerable experience with C++, going back to when it was called C with Classes and you used a preprocessor to convert C with Classes code to C and then compiled the C. What Linus says is pretty much true. I don't expect it to matter though since when people say "I'd be more interested on what someone who actually has a lot of experience in using C++ says." they don't really mean it. There is another guy I know of with considerable experience in C++, Yossi Kreinin, who knows more about C++ than Stroustrop.     droithomme, 2012 · comment · in Linus Torvalds on C++

  • If you want to learn more, I'd suggest at first reading (or at least skimming) Bjarne Stroustrup's "The C++ programming language". Also, the C++ FAQ http://www.parashift.com/c++-faq/ is useful for getting an overview on the corner cases of C++. After which you should visit its inverse, the C++ FQA (frequently questioned answers, i.e. an anti-C++ answer to the FAQ) http://www.yosefk.com/c++fqa/ to learn why no-one in their right mind would ever use C++. That won't teach you C++ per se, but would give you a useful look at the breadth of the language and how and why things happen the way they do.

    anonymous, 2013 · comment · in Everybody thinks about garbage collection the wrong way (2010)

  • > as there's so much more that can go wrong Uh. Everything that can go wrong in C can go wrong in C++ by definition. C++ also adds a lot of extra things that can go wrong which don't exist in C. There are plenty of arguments to be made for C++ over C but "so much more that can go wrong in C" is not one of them. The C++ FQA is strongly recommended reading: http://yosefk.com/c++fqa/

    nullc, 2013 · comment · in Why is BIND 10 written in C++ and Python?
