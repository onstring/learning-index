  • If you're going to be writing assembly on Linux, you should decide which of the two major syntaxes to learn: - AT&T (gas, gcc inline assembly) - Intel (nasm, fasm, yasm, but also gas) Some links to help you decide: Pro-gas: http://www.deater.net/weave/vmwprod/asm/ll/README.gas_sucks Anti-gas: http://x86asm.net/articles/what-i-dislike-about-gas/ Neither/both: http://www.ibm.com/developerworks/linux/library/l-gas-nasm/i... Neither: http://webster.cs.ucr.edu/AsmTools/WhichAsm.html If you decide to go with AT&T/gas, check out "Programming from the Ground Up" , by Jonathan Bartlett: http://download.savannah.gnu.org/releases/pgubook/ If you decide to go with Intel/nasm, see "PC Assembly Language"

    gnosis, 2011 · comment · in The Art of Assembly Language Programming (Linux edition) [book]
